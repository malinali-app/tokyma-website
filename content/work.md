Tokyma is a consultancy. We help research labs, NGOs, and language communities decide what is feasible, what data already exists, and which tools belong on a given pair. We also edit [Malinali](https://malinali.app/), a small offline translation app. The app is one place the advice becomes software. It is not the whole practice.

The industry around us is changing quickly. Cloud translation and large models now reach languages that sat outside commercial catalogues for years. That does not settle every question of register, orthography, confidentiality, or ownership. Those questions are usually why someone writes to us.

## Consulting

A typical engagement starts with the language pair and the constraint — health, media, research, a place with little connectivity — and whether any data is already in hand. The first reply is a reading of the landscape, not a proposal for a model.

From there the work is often inventory and hygiene: dictionaries, speech, scripture, news, radio, licences. Sometimes it is evaluation of an existing system against the register people actually use. Sometimes it is a fine-tune, packaging for a device, or a recommendation to join a community that has already done the collection. An honest no is a deliverable. So is “call [CLEAR Global](https://www.clearglobal.org/)” or “read [Masakhane](https://www.masakhane.io/) before you scrape.”

## Research

Low-resource translation is not only a missing API. It is data, orthography, dialect, and who gets to decide how a language is represented. Our [notes]({{< relref "/notes" >}}) follow that map: what the large platforms now cover, who already built corpora, where speech is ahead of text, and — further out — how the same class of tools is being used on manuscripts and on animal communication.

We publish the bibliography because the same forty-thousand-line corpus keeps being rediscovered.

## Dataset gathering

The job is slower than a download. Find what exists, and under which licence. Sit with speakers on register — news, radio, clinic, market, liturgy. Align, filter, and keep a hold-out that is not shuffled verses. Record more. Oral languages do not become text because a paper needed a table.

We work first in West and Central African languages because that is where we have spent the most time. The method is not geography-locked.
