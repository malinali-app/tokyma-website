Several industries use the word *translation* and almost never meet. Mixing them up is how procurement goes wrong.

[Masakhane](https://www.masakhane.io/) (“we build together”) is the gravitational centre of African NLP as a community rather than a vendor. Their early diagnosis still holds: African languages are under-funded, under-indexed, and too often published in venues that are hard to find from outside the continent. They treated machine translation as a stepping stone into NER, QA, and speech, and they are explicit about data sovereignty. Anyone starting a pair in West or Southern Africa should read their [dataset list](https://github.com/masakhane-io/masakhane-community/blob/master/list-of-datasets.md) and [AfriQA](https://github.com/masakhane-io/afriqa/tree/main/data/queries) before proposing a new crawl.

[CLEAR Global](https://www.clearglobal.org/) (still, in part, Translators without Borders) is what language access looks like when the user is in a camp, a clinic, or a flood. They talk in people unreached, not in BLEU. Their [language-use data by country](https://translatorswithoutborders.org/language-data-by-country/) is the map to open before assuming the official language is the one spoken at the window. Human, humanitarian, high-trust: sometimes the right next step is not a model.

[SIL Global](https://www.sil.org/) has been in orthography, literacy, and minority-language documentation since long before “low-resource” was a workshop track. Bloom speech checkpoints exist because SIL already had recording practice, not because a foundation needed a leaderboard. Around them sit [Lacuna Fund](https://lacunafund.org/datasets/language/), national labs, church and radio archives, university desks that never got a GitHub star.

A different aisle sells hardware. Market notes on “language translation devices” recycle the same logos — Pocketalk, Timekettle, Vasco, Langogo, Travis, Mymanu, Jarvisen, alongside the large platform vendors. They sell two-way conversation, a camera, a battery, a SKU. The languages on the box are the languages with APIs. That market is real. Travellers buy it. It is not a research programme, and it is not the same job as Masakhane or TWB.

The catalogues are filling in. The people who already collected the data are still the ones to read first.
